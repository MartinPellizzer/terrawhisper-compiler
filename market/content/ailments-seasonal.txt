Bronchitis (acute)
Strep Throat
Sinusitis

February
Common Cold
Chilblains (inflammatory skin condition)
Norovirus ("Winter Vomiting Bug")
Dry Cough (from cold air exposure)

March
Allergic Rhinitis (Spring Allergies)
Pink Eye (Viral Conjunctivitis)
Asthma Exacerbation (pollen-induced)
Eczema Flares (transition from dry winter)

April
Hay Fever (Late Spring Allergies)
Cold Sores (Herpes Simplex flare-ups)
Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD recovery phase)
Spring Fever (mild fatigue/irritability from changing seasons)

May
Tick-Borne Illnesses (e.g., Lyme Disease onset)
Seasonal Asthenia (Spring fatigue)
Poison Ivy/Oak Dermatitis
Mold Allergies

June
Sunburn (UV-induced skin inflammation)
Heat Rash (Miliaria)
Swimmer’s Ear (Otitis externa)
Food Poisoning (from picnics/barbecues)

July
Dehydration-related Fatigue

Why Seasonal: Hot summer days and sweating lead to fluid/electrolyte loss.

Herbs: Hibiscus, lemon balm, licorice (in moderation), nettle (mineral-rich)

Insect Bite Reactions

Why Seasonal: Mosquito and other insect populations peak in summer.

Herbs: Plantain, calendula, tea tree oil

Athlete’s Foot (Fungal infection)

Why Seasonal: Hot, moist conditions from sandals or closed shoes promote fungus growth.

Herbs: Tea tree oil, garlic, calendula, myrrh

Photodermatitis (Sun sensitivity rash)

Why Seasonal: UV rays cause abnormal skin reactions, especially in people taking certain medications.

Herbs: Aloe vera, St. John’s Wort (internal use may worsen sensitivity; topical only), lavender

August
Heat Exhaustion

Why Seasonal: Prolonged exposure to extreme heat and sun.

Herbs: Dandelion (replenishes minerals), lemon balm, hibiscus

Hives (Urticaria from heat or food)

Why Seasonal: Hot weather, stress, or outdoor allergens can trigger.

Herbs: Nettles, peppermint, licorice

Eye Irritation (from dust/pollen/swimming)

Why Seasonal: Outdoor and water activities peak.

Herbs: Eyebright, chamomile (as eyewash), calendula

Motion Sickness (from summer travel)

Why Seasonal: Increased car/boat/plane travel during vacation season.

Herbs: Ginger, peppermint, lemon balm

September
Back-to-School Colds

Why Seasonal: Children return to school and share germs in crowded settings.

Herbs: Echinacea, elderberry, astragalus, thyme

Ragweed Allergy (Fall allergic rhinitis)

Why Seasonal: Ragweed pollen peaks in early fall.

Herbs: Nettle, butterbur, goldenrod

Fall Asthma Flares

Why Seasonal: Pollen, mold, and cold air spikes worsen respiratory symptoms.

Herbs: Mullein, licorice root, turmeric

Cold-Induced Joint Pain

Why Seasonal: Temperature drops begin to impact inflammation and circulation.

Herbs: Ginger, turmeric, willow bark

October
Seasonal Affective Disorder (onset)

Why Seasonal: Shortening daylight affects mood and melatonin levels.

Herbs: St. John’s Wort, Rhodiola, ashwagandha (monitor for interactions)

Dry Skin (Autumn onset)

Why Seasonal: Air becomes cooler and drier, stripping skin of moisture.

Herbs: Calendula, oatstraw, chickweed (usually applied topically)

Cracked Lips and Hands

Why Seasonal: Windy, dry weather and indoor heating begin to affect hydration.

Herbs: Calendula, comfrey, shea butter (herbal-infused)

Cold-Induced Headaches

Why Seasonal: Shifts in barometric pressure and colder temps can trigger migraines.

Herbs: Feverfew, peppermint, ginger

November
Cold Sores (recurrence in cold weather)

Why Seasonal: Weakened immunity and UV/chill stress on lips.

Herbs: Lemon balm, lysine (from herbal sources), licorice root

Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV)

Why Seasonal: Spreads in cooler weather among infants and the elderly.

Herbs: Elderberry, licorice root, thyme (symptom relief only)

Sore Throats (due to dry air and colds)

Why Seasonal: Early indoor heating dries mucous membranes.

Herbs: Slippery elm, marshmallow root, sage

Sleep Disruption (light/dark cycle changes)

Why Seasonal: Shorter days impact circadian rhythm.

Herbs: Valerian, chamomile, passionflower

December
Holiday Digestive Issues (overeating, stress)

Why Seasonal: Rich foods, alcohol, and stress are common during holidays.

Herbs: Peppermint, ginger, fennel, dandelion

Winter Depression ("Holiday Blues")

Why Seasonal: Social pressure, loneliness, and low sunlight contribute.

Herbs: Rhodiola, St. John’s Wort, lavender

RSV & Flu Resurgence

Why Seasonal: Increased travel and gatherings spread viruses.

Herbs: Elderberry, echinacea, thyme (again, supportive use)

Cracked Heels (from indoor heating/dryness)

Why Seasonal: Cold weather and dry indoor environments cause skin splitting.

Herbs: Calendula, comfrey, plantain (as salves or soaks)
